# Story 9: The Whole Truth
## Part One: A Family Decision

**[PLATE 1 — quiet camp life some days after Ch39, Robin noticing how much lighter things feel now that he knows]**

The days after Crane's visit settled into something Robin hadn't quite expected — not merely relief that the danger had passed, but a particular, unfamiliar lightness he kept catching himself noticing at odd moments, the way a person notices the absence of a persistent ache only once it's finally gone.

He watched Thistlewood mending a torn sleeve by the fire, staff propped unremarkably against the wall, an entirely ordinary old man once more, and found himself smiling at the sight in a way that would have been impossible only weeks before.

"You look pleased about something," Marian observed, settling beside him with her own quiet amusement.

"I keep noticing how much easier everything is," Robin admitted, "now that I'm not carrying half-formed questions around with me every hour of the day. I hadn't realized how much weight that was, until it was gone."

"That's rather the nature of secrets," Marian said. "You don't feel the weight of carrying one properly until you've finally set it down."

Robin was quiet a moment, turning something over with more care than he usually gave his own half-formed thoughts. "Will and Long John and Crumb are still carrying questions, though," he said, finally. "Smaller ones, maybe — they've never let themselves suspect what I did. But they've noticed things. The way you and Thistlewood sometimes seem to understand each other without speaking. The way I've been different, since Crane's visit, and nobody's properly explained why."

"You're thinking of telling them," Marian said. Not a question.

"I'm thinking I'd like to," Robin said. "I know it's not simply my choice to make. But I wanted you to hear the thought first, before I said it to anyone else."

---

**[PLATE 2 — a real, vulnerable conversation between Robin and Marian, genuine emotional openness]**

"Why me first?" Marian asked, though something in her voice suggested she already suspected the answer and simply wanted to hear him say it plainly.

"Because you're the one who's carried this longest," Robin said. "Because whatever we decide, I trust your judgment on it more than my own. And because—" He hesitated, something in his usual easy confidence giving way to real, unguarded honesty. "Because I find I want to be honest with you about rather more than just this, lately. Knowing the truth about Thistlewood didn't only change what I understand about him. It changed what I'm willing to say aloud, generally. About all sorts of things I'd been quietly carrying without properly naming."

Marian looked at him for a long moment, something warm and careful working behind her usual composure. "That's a rather large admission to make in the middle of a conversation about Will's feelings, Robin."

"I'm aware," Robin said, with a small, rueful laugh. "I find I'm rather bad at doing only one honest thing at a time, lately."

"For what it's worth," Marian said, quieter now, something in her own careful composure softening in a way Robin had rarely seen from her, "I've noticed the same thing in myself. Wanting to say things plainly, rather than filing them away for some safer day that never quite arrives." She held his gaze, steady, warm, entirely unhurried. "I don't think either of us needs to solve that today, on top of everything else we're already deciding. But I wanted you to know I'd noticed it too."

Robin felt something settle in his chest, warm and unresolved and entirely welcome. "Then we'll leave it there," he said, "for today. One honest thing at a time, perhaps, after all."

"One at a time," Marian agreed, and the smile she gave him carried more warmth than either of them quite trusted themselves to examine further, just yet.

---

**[PLATE 3 — Robin and Marian approaching Thistlewood and Pip, genuine consent-seeking rather than unilateral decision]**

They found Thistlewood and Pip together at the cottage, and Robin, despite having rehearsed the conversation more than once in his own head, found the actual asking considerably harder than the rehearsing had suggested it would be.

"I'd like to tell Will, Long John, and Crumb the truth," he said, plainly, once they'd settled. "About you. About Pip. I know it's not solely my decision — it's your secret as much as it's now mine, and I wouldn't dream of making this choice without both of you properly agreeing to it first."

Thistlewood set down his work, considering this with real, unhurried gravity. "That's a great deal of trust to extend to three more people," he said. "Even good people. Even people who've already proven themselves, a dozen times over, in every way that generally matters."

"I know," Robin said. "I'm not asking lightly. I simply don't think we're meant to be a family with quiet corners in it. Not anymore. Not after everything we've all survived together."

Pip, who had been listening with real attentiveness, finally spoke. "I think he's right," she said, to Thistlewood, gently. "I was so frightened of this, once. The idea of anyone else knowing. But Marian's known for ages now, and nothing broke. Robin knows, and nothing broke. I think I'd rather trust the rest of them properly than keep performing a disguise in front of people who love us regardless of what's underneath it."

Thistlewood was quiet a long moment, something old and considering working across his weathered features. "You're both very likely right," he said, finally. "I find myself simply frightened in the particular way an old man gets frightened, after guarding something so carefully for so long that guarding it starts to feel like the only shape safety can properly take." He looked between them, something warm settling into his expression despite the lingering caution. "But I trust your judgment, Robin. And I trust Pip's. If you both believe this is right, I won't stand in the way of it."

"Then we're agreed," Marian said, quiet satisfaction in her voice. "Now we simply need to decide how, and when."
