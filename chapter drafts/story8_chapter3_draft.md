# Story 8: The Witchfinder's Patience
## Part Three: Countermeasures

**[PLATE 1 — the crew planning a proactive response, options debated]**

"We can't simply wait for him to return a third time," Marian said, once they'd regrouped at camp, the whole shape of the problem laid out before them with the particular careful precision she brought to anything genuinely dangerous. "Waiting means he chooses the terms. We need to choose them instead."

"Choose them how?" Robin asked. "We can't exactly ask him to stop being suspicious."

"No," Marian agreed. "But we might give him somewhere else to point that suspicion. A man assembling a theory from scattered pieces can be redirected, if the pieces start pointing somewhere more convenient than the truth."

"You want to build him a different old man to chase," Will said, slowly, understanding dawning. "A decoy."

"Something like that," Marian said. "Though it'll want careful handling, and I suspect it'll want your particular talents more than mine."

"There's a hermit," Ferret Tam offered, arriving mid-conversation with the particular casual usefulness that had become, by now, simply how he entered rooms. "Genuine one, out past the eastern district. Keeps to himself, odd habits, the kind of fellow who's already collected his fair share of strange stories without any help from us. If word reached Crane that there's *another* unusual old man worth his patience, considerably further from here than Thistlewood's cottage—"

"He might split his attention," Marian finished, already turning the shape of it over. "Or redirect it entirely, if the story's compelling enough."

"It feels rather unkind," Robin said, "pointing a Witchfinder at an innocent man."

"It's considerably kinder than the alternative," Marian said, "provided we're careful he comes to no actual harm from it. Rumor alone, properly managed, shouldn't cost him anything but a wasted afternoon."

---

**[PLATE 2 — executing the plan: Will and Tam working their connections to spread the misdirection]**

Will and Tam set to work with the particular easy competence of men who had, between them, spent considerable portions of their lives managing precisely this kind of delicate social engineering — a word dropped here, a rumor seeded there, always secondhand, always plausible, always carrying just enough specific, colorful detail to be worth a patient investigator's time.

"The trick," Will explained, to Robin, who had insisted on coming along if only to understand how the thing actually worked, "isn't lying outright. It's arranging the truth so it points somewhere other than where it started. That hermit genuinely is odd. People genuinely have talked about him. We're simply making certain the *right* people happen to mention it to the right listening ears."

"That's rather more sophisticated than I expected from you," Robin admitted.

"I'm a man of many unexpected depths," Will said, "most of which involve considerable practice at exactly this sort of thing."

By evening, the rumor had properly taken root — three separate conversations, artfully unconnected, all pointing toward the same eastern hermit with just enough colorful specificity to be worth a patient man's attention.

*"It's rather elegant, actually,"* Grimalkin observed, watching from a rooftop with genuine professional appreciation. *"Using the truth as the lie's foundation. Considerably harder to unravel than something invented from nothing."*

*"I hope the hermit doesn't mind too much,"* Barley said, worried. *"Being talked about like that, without knowing it's happening."*

*"He'll mind considerably less than Pip would, if the alternative worked out badly,"* Grimalkin said, and Barley, weighing this, found he didn't have much of an argument against it.

---

**[PLATE 3 — parallel escalation: signs that Crane has made independent progress, tension of the race against time]**

It was Wat, arriving breathless the following morning, who brought word that considerably complicated the crew's cautious optimism.

"Crane's been talking to the miller," he reported. "The one whose cart got stopped near Sherwood, back when — well, back before I properly understood what all of it meant. He's cross-referencing accounts now. Comparing what different people remember, looking for the pieces that match up."

"That's considerably more methodical than a man simply collecting rumors," Marian said, grim. "That's a man building a timeline."

"Does he know about the hermit yet?" Robin asked.

"Not that anyone's said," Wat admitted. "But he's asking sharper questions than he was a week ago. Whatever's slowing him down, it isn't confusion."

---

Marian absorbed this with the particular careful stillness she reserved for genuinely difficult news, already recalculating the shape of a plan that had, until a moment ago, felt considerably more comfortable than it currently did.

"We're not ahead of him," she said, finally. "We're running alongside him, at best, hoping our redirection reaches his attention before his own methodical work reaches its natural conclusion." She looked around at the gathered group. "That's not nothing. But it's considerably less comfortable than I'd hoped this plan would leave us."

"Then we make certain the hermit story reaches him properly," Will said, grim now himself, all trace of his earlier easy confidence gone. "Today, not eventually. However that needs arranging."

---

**[PLATE 4 — closing: uncertain outcome, real tension carried forward]**

They spent the rest of the day making certain of exactly that — Tam calling in favors he'd clearly been saving for something considerably more comfortable than this, Will charming his way through conversations with a focus that left no room for his usual vanity, the whole effort carrying the particular grim urgency of people racing a clock they couldn't actually see the face of.

By nightfall, there was nothing further to be done but wait, and hope, and trust that a rumor properly planted would find its way to exactly the ears that needed it before a patient man's own careful methods reached the conclusion they'd been quietly building toward all along.

---

*(new text page)*

"Did it work?" Robin asked, later, watching Marian stare into the fire with an intensity that suggested her mind was considerably further away than the flames in front of her.

"I don't know," Marian admitted, which cost her something to say plainly, given how rarely she permitted herself that particular uncertainty aloud. "We've done what we could. Whether it's enough, whether it reaches him in time, whether he even follows it once it does—" She shook her head. "We'll know soon enough. That's rather the trouble with plans like this. You plant them, and then you simply have to live with not knowing, until the answer arrives on its own schedule instead of yours."

Somewhere beyond the firelight, in a district none of them could presently see, a patient man continued his careful work, entirely unaware of the rumor now quietly chasing his attention — and whether it would catch him in time was a question none of them, for all their considerable effort, could yet properly answer.
