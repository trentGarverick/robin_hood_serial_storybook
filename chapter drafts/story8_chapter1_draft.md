# Story 8: The Witchfinder's Patience
## Part One: Too Close to Home

*(Working titles — happy to change either. First of a 6-part story — this one touches the central secret directly, please read carefully.)*

---

**[PLATE 1 — camp life settling into an uneasy new normal, the "borrowed time" tension from Story 7 still present]**

Life had gone on, in the weeks since Ferret Tam's cellar had been emptied and refilled with considerably duller goods than before, in the particular watchful quiet of people who understood that a danger merely delayed was not the same thing as a danger resolved.

"Any word?" Robin asked, for perhaps the third time that week, watching Marian return from another quiet meeting with one of Tam's contacts.

"Nothing new," Marian said, which had become, by now, its own small ritual of reassurance neither of them entirely believed. "Crane's still asking questions. Still patient about it. We've simply stopped being the only thing he's asking about, for the moment."

"That's not the same as stopping," Robin said.

"No," Marian agreed. "It never was."

---

*(new text page)*

It was Ferret Tam himself who broke the uneasy quiet, arriving at camp with none of his usual careful unhurriedness, dismounting before his horse had properly stopped.

"I need you to listen to this properly," he said, without preamble, "because I don't like the shape of what I'm about to tell you, and I like considerably less that I'm the one telling it."

"What's happened?" Marian asked, already alert.

"Crane's had a name," Tam said. "A proper one, this time. Not 'an old man out Sherwood way.' *Thistlewood.* Someone gave it to him — I don't know who, and I'd rather not speculate until I do — and a rough sense of the district his cottage sits in besides."

---

**[PLATE 2 — the crew's alarm, understanding the severity of this specific escalation]**

The silence that followed had real weight to it, the particular stillness of people recognizing, all at once, precisely how much distance had just closed between a dangerous rumor and an actionable target.

"A name," Will repeated, flat. "He has an actual name now."

"And a district," Tam confirmed, grim. "Not the exact cottage, not yet, so far as my sources could tell me. But considerably closer than 'somewhere in a very large forest.' A patient man with a name and a district doesn't stay far from a door for long."

"We need to warn him," Robin said, already moving. "Today. Now, if we can manage it."

---

*(new text page)*

"Warn him of what, exactly," Marian said, though there was no real argument in it, only the careful precision of a woman already turning the problem over from every necessary angle. "That a Witchfinder has his name? Thistlewood likely knows more about the shape of that danger than any of us could tell him. What he needs isn't a warning. It's help deciding what to actually do about it."

"Then we bring him the name," Long John said, "and let him decide with the whole picture in front of him, rather than guessing at pieces the way the rest of us have been forced to."

Brother Crumb, uncharacteristically quiet through the exchange, finally spoke. "Whatever he decides, I'd rather we decided it together. He's carried enough of this alone for one lifetime."

---

**[PLATE 3 — preparing to travel to Thistlewood's cottage, urgency mixed with careful planning]**

They set out within the hour, the particular grim efficiency of people who had learned, across a long and difficult year, precisely how much speed a genuine crisis actually required without tipping over into the kind of carelessness that created new ones.

*"This is different,"* Grimalkin said, low, to the other two, uncharacteristically serious. *"Every other danger we've weathered, we've weathered as a group, together, out here. This one's aimed somewhere else. Somewhere none of us can properly stand between him and it, if it comes to that."*

*"He's not helpless,"* Barley said, though something in his usual easy confidence had thinned considerably. *"He's never been helpless."*

*"No,"* Grimalkin agreed. *"But he's also never had a name attached to a district before. That's a different kind of danger than anything he's had to manage until now."*

---

*(new text page)*

Marian, walking near the front of the group, found herself turning the whole shape of the danger over with the particular careful attention she gave everything that actually mattered — not merely the immediate risk of discovery, but the deeper, harder question underneath it: what happened to all of them, to Robin especially, if the answer to Crane's patient assembling turned out to be worse than any of them had properly prepared for.

"We'll reach the cottage by evening," she said, mostly to herself, checking the angle of the sun with practiced ease. "Whatever we find there, whatever needs deciding — better we're the ones standing beside him when he decides it, rather than leaving him to face a Witchfinder's patience entirely alone."

Robin, walking beside her, said nothing, though the particular set of his jaw suggested he had already decided, privately, exactly how far he intended to go to make certain that didn't happen.

---

**[PLATE 4 — arriving near the cottage, first signs that Crane's investigation has already progressed further than feared]**

They smelled the smoke before they saw the cottage itself — not the cottage burning, thankfully, only an ordinary hearth-fire, but the particular tension of arriving somewhere already braced for considerably worse than they found waiting for them.

Pip met them at the treeline, and there was something in her stance — alert, tightly controlled, entirely unlike her usual easy confidence — that told Marian, before a single word had passed between them, that whatever news they'd carried this far had already, somehow, arrived ahead of them.

"You've heard," Marian said.

"We've had a visitor," Pip said, grim. "Two days past. A quiet man, asking about herbs and remedies, terribly interested in how long we'd lived out this way." She looked back toward the cottage, where a thin line of smoke rose steady and ordinary against the evening sky. "He didn't give a name. I don't think he needed to. I'd wager considerable coin he already had ours."

---

*(new text page)*

"Crane," Robin said, the word landing heavier than he'd intended it to.

"Almost certainly," Pip agreed. "Thistlewood's inside. He's been rather quiet since the visit, which is not, I promise you, a good sign where he's concerned. I think he's known this was coming for longer than either of us have properly discussed aloud."

Marian looked toward the cottage, toward the ordinary curl of smoke rising from a chimney that suddenly felt considerably less safe than it had an hour ago, and felt the full, cold weight of exactly how little distance remained between the world they'd carefully protected and the one patiently closing in around it.

"Then we'd best go in," she said, "and find out exactly how bad this actually is."
