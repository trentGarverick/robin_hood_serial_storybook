# Story 3: The Devil's Own Luck
## Part One: A Change of Scenery

**[PLATE 1 — Thistlewood, Pip, and the three animals arriving at Sherwood Camp, warm reunion, the gang delighted]**

They heard Barley before they saw any of them, which had become, in the months since the cottage, a fairly reliable rule of thumb. The dog came crashing through the undergrowth at a dead sprint, ears flying, and reached Brother Crumb with such total enthusiasm that the two of them went down together in an undignified heap that neither seemed even slightly bothered by.

"Well," said Thistlewood, arriving at a more dignified pace with Pip and the other two animals in tow, surveying the scene with the particular fondness of a man pretending not to have missed something. "I see standards remain as high as ever."

"Higher, if anything," said Marian, already crossing to greet them, and if her eyes lingered a half-second longer on Pip than strict courtesy required, she gave no sign of having noticed it herself.

Grimalkin, for his part, wasted no time on preamble, making directly for Marian's shoulders with the proprietary confidence of a cat reclaiming property that had merely been on loan.

"Miss me?" he asked Gideon, once he'd settled, in the low private register that carried, as it always did, to no ears but the animals' own.

"I managed," said Gideon, unbothered, already investigating the contents of an unattended sack with professional interest.

"You always say that. You always mean the opposite."

"I contain multitudes," said Gideon, which was as close as he generally came to admitting anything.

---

**[PLATE 2 — the reunion settling into easy conversation and catching up, Robin practicing archery nearby with his usual results]**

Robin, meanwhile, had drifted toward the practice target with the particular sheepishness of a man who knew exactly how this was going to go and had decided to do it anyway rather than admit as much aloud. He drew. He loosed. The arrow sailed well wide of the target and buried itself, with a small definitive *thock*, directly into a low branch — which, as it happened, was the exact branch a wasps' nest had been quietly expanding on for the better part of a week, and which chose that moment to express its displeasure at considerable volume.

"I didn't do that on purpose," Robin said, already backing away.

"Nobody thinks you did anything on purpose, dear," said Marian, not unkindly, already moving to put several trees between herself and the wasps. "That's rather the point of you."

"He's improving," Pip offered, watching the retreat with open amusement.

"He is not," said Will, from a safe distance, checking his sleeve out of long habit rather than any real concern. "He is exactly as bad as he's always been. He's simply been bad at it long enough that we've stopped being surprised."

"There's a word for that," said Marian.

"Endurance?"

"Tenure," said Marian, and even Robin, extracting himself from the wasps' general vicinity with as much dignity as the situation allowed, had to laugh at that one.

---

**[PLATE 3 — the alarm: Vane and his guards approaching the camp, tension breaking the warm mood]**

It was Gideon who noticed first, which surprised no one who knew him — his head came up mid-chew, ears forward, an entirely different animal from the one who'd been placidly investigating sacks a moment before.

"Company," he said, to the other two, flat and certain. "Armed. A dozen, near enough. And I know that walk."

"Whose walk?" asked Barley, already alert, already low to the ground in a way that suggested play had stopped being the point of the afternoon.

"The manor," said Gideon. "The one who shouted."

Grimalkin's ears flattened. "Tell the humans. Now."

There was no time for the animals' warning to reach the gang through any means but the obvious one — Gideon simply put himself between the treeline and the camp and began, without further preamble, headbutting the nearest available object with enough force to be heard.

"That's new," said Long John, watching a goat attempt to relocate a supply crate through sheer commitment.

"That's *not* new," said Marian, already reaching for her bow, some instinct older than reason telling her the goat's alarm and the sudden crack of a twig in the treeline were not unrelated events. "That's Gideon telling us something's wrong. Everyone, *now*—"

Vane stepped out of the trees before she finished the sentence, a dozen armed men fanning out behind him, and any remaining warmth in the afternoon evaporated at once.

---

**[PLATE 4 — chaos erupting: Thistlewood fighting discreetly with his staff, Robin's chaotic arrow, general melee]**

"You," Vane said, looking directly at Marian with the particular satisfaction of a man who had spent considerable time anticipating this exact sentence. "I told you the account wasn't closed."

What followed did not resemble a fair fight, though not for any reason Vane's men could have anticipated. Thistlewood moved through the chaos with his staff and his unhurried, unbothered calm, disarming men with strikes that looked, to anyone watching closely enough to wonder, rather more precise than an old man's reflexes ought to allow — though nobody present had the spare attention to wonder very hard. Robin, cornered by two of Vane's men, loosed an arrow in desperation that missed both of them entirely and instead severed the rope holding a heavy provisions net aloft, which descended directly onto the two men with the comprehensive suddenness of a very effective trap neither Robin nor anyone else had actually planned.

"I don't understand how that keeps happening," Robin said, staring at the net.

"Don't," said Marian, already moving past him. "Just be grateful."

*"Left flank,"* said Grimalkin, somewhere above the fray, in the clipped efficient tone of an animal with no time for banter. *"Two of them, moving on the giant's blind side."*

*"On it,"* said Barley, and was gone before the sentence finished, a streak of teeth and enthusiasm that turned two advancing guards into two rapidly retreating ones within the space of a few seconds.

*"Show-off,"* said Grimalkin, though there was something almost like approval buried in it.

*"You're just annoyed I moved first,"* said Barley, from somewhere in the chaos, entirely too pleased with himself to be properly chastised.

---

**[PLATE 5 — Gideon's temper-flip in full effect: dramatic transformation, headbutting guards, guards terrified, crying witchcraft]**

It was Gideon, in the end, who ended the argument entirely — struck, not badly, by a guard who mistook a farm animal for an obstacle rather than a participant, and who discovered, in the half-second that followed, precisely how large a miscalculation that had been.

The goat that turned on him bore only a passing resemblance to the placid creature that had been investigating sacks twenty minutes earlier. He came in low and fast, put the guard on his back with a single furious headbutt, and turned immediately to the next nearest target with an expression that several witnesses would later independently describe, without any prompting from one another, as *personal*.

"*Witchcraft!*" someone screamed — not entirely unreasonably, given a goat had just felled a grown man twice its weight without visible effort — and the cry spread through Vane's men with the particular speed that panic always seems to find first.

---

*(new text page)*

Vane himself did not run immediately, which was, in its way, the most frightening thing about him — he stood a moment longer than his men, staring at Marian across the chaos with an expression that had stopped being merely angry and settled into something colder and more patient.

"This isn't finished," he said.

"It rarely is, with you," said Marian, bow steady, refusing to give him the satisfaction of looking away first.

He left without further ceremony, dragging his last few men with him, and the sound of their retreat through the underbrush had the distinct, undignified quality of men who intended to tell this story very differently to anyone who asked.

---

**[PLATE 6 — aftermath: the camp settling, Thistlewood's cottage announcement, warm closing, Marian's private suspicion]**

"A right widow's-mite of an afternoon," said Brother Crumb, into the settling quiet, already assessing the damage to his cooking pot with visible relief that it had survived.

Robin frowned. "A widow's—"

"*Fright*, Robin," said the entire camp, in the ragged, affectionate unison that had, by now, become less a running joke than a fixed point of the universe.

Thistlewood, dabbing at a small cut above one eyebrow with the unbothered air of a man for whom this was a distinctly minor inconvenience, cleared his throat once everyone had more or less caught their breath.

"I've leased a small cottage," he said, "not far from here, actually — I find I'm in want of a change of scenery, and Pip agrees the old place has gotten rather dull. We'll be in the area a good while, I expect." He glanced, with something almost sheepish for a man his age, toward Gideon, Grimalkin, and Barley, all three currently receiving the enthusiastic gratitude of five very relieved outlaws. "I don't suppose the three of them might spend some time here, from time to time. They seem to have taken rather a liking to the company."

"They can stay as long as they like," said Long John, still catching his breath, scratching behind Barley's ears with real feeling. "After that? They've more than earned their keep."

Marian said nothing for a moment, watching Thistlewood tend his own wound with hands that moved with far too much certainty for a man his age, watching Pip stand close by with the same unreadable watchfulness she'd noticed a dozen times now and never once mentioned aloud. She filed it away, as she always did, with everything else she wasn't yet ready to say out loud — the fight, the staff, the boy who moved like he'd trained for it his whole short life and somehow, despite that, still seemed younger than he ought to be in ways she couldn't quite name.

"Welcome back," was all she said, and meant it, and left the rest for later.
