# Story: Six Degrees of Sherwood
## Part Three: Will Scarlett-Sleeves

*(Working title — happy to change.)*

---

**[PLATE 1 — Robin and Long John cutting Will down from the tree, Will landing awkwardly but immediately checking his sleeve]**

Cutting a man down from a tree is, as a rule, a fairly simple undertaking, and it would have remained one had the man in question not spent the entire process more concerned with the state of his sleeve than the state of his neck.

"Mind the — no, the *other* side, you'll — " Will Scarlett-Sleeves landed, at last, in an undignified heap, and was on his feet checking his cuff again before Robin had finished coiling the rope. "Ah. Good. Still intact." He said this with the enormous relief of a man reporting a genuine medical recovery.

"You were upside down in a forest," said Long John.

"I was upside down in a forest *with excellent posture*," said Will, "which is more than most people manage right side up. Will Scarlett-Sleeves. Delighted, truly." He extended a hand that had, moments ago, been six feet off the ground.

---

**[PLATE 2 — flashback begins: Will in fine clothes at a tavern or market stall, buying something extravagant he can't afford, charming and confident]**

Which brought them, as these things generally did, to the matter of how a young man ends up hanging from a tree in the first place — a story Will told with the unhurried pride of a man who considered the *getting-into-trouble* part the interesting bit, and the consequences merely a technicality that had happened to him rather than a thing he'd done.

He had, by his own cheerful account, always understood the importance of appearances. A man who looked prosperous, he reasoned, was halfway to being prosperous — an economic theory that had worked well right up until the point where it required him to actually be prosperous, which was the half he had somewhat neglected. There had been coats. There had been a truly excellent pair of boots, since sold to someone with more patience for haggling than sense. There had been, above all, the sleeve — scarlet, absurdly fine, acquired at a cost he described as "reasonable" in the same tone other men used for words like "unavoidable."

"A man's got to invest in himself," Will explained, examining the sleeve now for a third time, apparently satisfied it had survived the tree.

---

**[PLATE 3 — flashback: a dice or cards game at a tavern table, Will confidently wagering, other players watching, Ferret Tam present at the edge of the scene]**

The trouble, as trouble tends to, arrived once the coin for all this investing had run out, at which point Will had done what he considered the only sensible thing: doubled down, at a card table, with money that was not, strictly, his to be doubling.

He lost. He lost again, in the specific way that only makes sense to a man convinced his luck is due any moment, and by the time he'd finished losing he owed a sum he described, with real optimism, as "not insurmountable" to a wiry, sharp-eyed trader who went by Ferret Tam and had a reputation for collecting debts with considerably more reliability than he had for anything else.

"He seemed a reasonable sort," Will said. "Right up until he wasn't."

---

**[PLATE 4 — present day: Will finishing the story, Robin puzzled by cant, Long John unimpressed]**

"So the tree," Robin said slowly, "was—"

"A reminder," said Will. "Tam's men aren't unkind, as such. They simply believe a debt looks better from an unusual angle." He shrugged, an impressive feat given he was still holding his cut wrist at an angle to inspect it for rope burn. "I am, if we're being precise about it, thoroughly pig-in-a-poke at present. Haven't a coin to my name."

Robin frowned. "Pig in a—"

"*Broke*, Robin," said Will and Long John, at exactly the same moment, in exactly the same weary tone, and then looked at each other with the faint, surprised solidarity of two men discovering a shared burden.

---

**[PLATE 5 — Ferret Tam's collectors arriving through the trees, more organized and businesslike than the tax man's searchers, closing in]**

It was, in retrospect, an extremely poorly timed sentence to have just finished, because it was at that exact moment that several men who were unmistakably *not* lost travelers stepped out of the treeline with the brisk, businesslike air of people who had been sent to finish a piece of unfinished business and intended to be home before supper.

"Scarlett," said the lead man, in the tone of someone who had said a great many names in exactly that tone. "Tam sends his regards. And an invoice."

"Do give him my compliments on the penmanship," said Will, already backing up.

---

**[PLATE 6 — three-way chaos: Robin's arrow, Long John's bulk, and Will's unexpected contribution (perhaps using his sleeve or quick wit) all combining]**

What followed was, by the increasingly low standards of the evening, almost coordinated. Robin's arrow went where Robin's arrows went, which was not at anyone in particular, but did succeed in cutting loose a hanging beehive someone had, for reasons lost to history, left dangling from a branch directly above the collectors. Long John, given a target he could actually see, simply stood in the middle of the path and became, for all practical purposes, a wall. And Will — cornered, out of plans, and apparently unwilling to let a crisis interfere with his composure — whipped off his fine scarlet sleeve and threw it, with surprising accuracy, directly over the lead collector's head.

It should not have worked. By any reasonable accounting, a sleeve is not a weapon. But a man who suddenly cannot see is a man who trips over roots, and a man tripping over roots in front of a wall the approximate size and shape of Long John Little is a man who has had, on balance, a very bad afternoon.

The sleeve, retrieved a moment later from the ground, proved — as it always would, though none of them would think to remark on it — entirely undamaged.

---

**[PLATE 7 — the three of them regrouping, and discovering Brother Crumb fast asleep nearby, having missed the entire commotion]**

They found the friar some while later, once the bees and the shouting had both faded into the middle distance, fast asleep against a tree with the profound, untroubled stillness of a man who had, by some miracle of constitution, slept through the entire affair.

"Is he," Long John began, carefully, "breathing?"

"Snoring," said Robin, "quite loudly, actually."

Will crouched down for a closer look, more out of curiosity than concern, and found, tucked against the friar's chest and guarded even in sleep, the unmistakable, flattened shape of what had recently been a meat pie.

"Well," said Will, straightening his sleeve one final time. "I do like a man with his priorities in order."
