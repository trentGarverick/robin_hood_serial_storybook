# Story 4: The Company She Keeps
## Part Three: The Hammer's Shadow

**[PLATE 1 — camp life continuing, the new normal settling in, Marian and Thistlewood's easy collaboration now an accepted (if unexplained) fixture]**

Life at Sherwood Camp had absorbed the change the way it absorbed most things, eventually — not with understanding, exactly, but with the particular practical acceptance of people who had learned, across enough strange months, that some questions simply stopped being worth the asking.

"They're doing it again," Will observed, watching Marian and Thistlewood conclude an entire tactical discussion in what appeared to be four sentences and a shared look, neither of which had included the rest of the group in any meaningful way.

"Doing what?" asked Brother Crumb, not looking up from his cooking.

"Being *specific* at each other," Will said, with the air of a man who had given up trying to name the phenomenon more precisely than that. "It's been happening for weeks."

"Works, though," said Long John, which was, by now, the entire camp's collectively agreed-upon final word on the matter.

Pip had noticed it too, from her usual quiet vantage near the fire, and found — somewhat to her own surprise — that watching Marian and Thistlewood fall into easy, unspoken coordination didn't sting the way she'd half-expected it to. If anything, it settled something in her that had been unsettled for longer than she'd let herself admit.

"You're less careful around her," Thistlewood observed, later, quietly, when the others weren't listening. "Around Marian, I mean."

"I don't have to measure every word anymore," Pip said. "It's strange, how much room that frees up. I hadn't realized how much of myself I'd been keeping folded away, just to make certain nothing slipped."

"No," Thistlewood agreed, with the particular gentleness he reserved for exactly these moments. "I don't imagine you had."

---

**[PLATE 2 — a larger, organized search party spotted approaching, more serious than a lone scout, the whole camp mobilizing]**

It was Gideon, unsurprisingly, who broke the peace — arriving at a dead run, no theater wasted on half-measures this time, straight past the fire and into the middle of camp, where he began shoving at Marian's leg with his head, insistent, in a way that bore no resemblance to his usual placid self.

*"Not one man this time,"* he said, to the other two, sharp and immediate. *"A dozen, moving in proper formation, and they know roughly where to look. This isn't scouting anymore. This is a search."*

Marian couldn't have told anyone what Gideon actually meant by any of it — his words weren't hers to hear — but she'd learned, across months of watching him, exactly what real alarm looked like on a creature who rarely bothered performing anything he didn't mean. She was on her feet and moving toward the treeline before she'd fully decided to.

"Something's coming," she said, "and it's serious. Look sharp." A moment later, cresting the low rise, she saw it for herself — a dozen men at least, moving in disciplined formation, close enough now that there'd be no misdirecting them with luck alone. "There. Just as Gideon meant."

*"Close enough that theatre won't buy us much time,"* said Grimalkin, already gone from her shoulders, already low and focused in a way that meant business. *"We need the woman properly involved this time, not just the old one."*

It was the first time all three of them — Marian, Pip, and Thistlewood — had needed to coordinate openly at once, and the shift in Pip was immediate and striking: gone entirely was the careful, deliberately-diminished boy the rest of the camp knew, replaced by someone quick, decisive, and plainly, thoroughly capable, directing Long John toward a blind approach with the crisp authority of someone who had clearly done considerably more of this than anyone watching had ever suspected.

"Since when does *Pip* give orders?" Robin asked, bewildered, already moving to comply regardless.

"Since always, apparently," Will said, catching up. "We simply weren't paying attention."

---

**[PLATE 3 — the three-way coordination working seamlessly: Marian's tactics, Pip's combat skill, Thistlewood's subtle help, evading and outmaneuvering the search party]**

What followed was, by a wide margin, the cleanest evasion the camp had ever managed — Marian's read on the terrain, Pip's quick, decisive redirection of the group's approach, and Thistlewood's quiet, barely-noticeable interventions (a root that wasn't quite where a searcher expected it, a bird startled at precisely the useful moment) combining into something considerably more coordinated than lucky improvisation had ever managed on its own.

The search party, misdirected twice and increasingly uncertain of their own bearings, eventually withdrew without ever having come within sight of the actual camp — which nobody but Marian, Pip, and Thistlewood fully understood to be the result of deliberate skill rather than the crew's usual improbable fortune.

"That was different," Robin said afterward, turning it over slowly. "Better. I don't quite know how to say what I mean by that."

"Does it need saying?" Marian asked, mild, already moving on.

Later, once the camp had settled and the others had drifted to their usual evening business, Pip found Marian alone at the fire's edge, and sat beside her without quite needing an invitation.

"Thank you," Pip said. "For not making me choose, back there, between being useful and being careful. I've spent so long managing which version of myself was allowed to show up in front of people. It was — it was rather wonderful, honestly, not having to."

"You don't owe me thanks for that," Marian said. "You never did."

"No," Pip agreed. "But I'm giving it anyway. You're allowed to simply take it."

---

**[PLATE 4 — a messenger or contact arriving with credible news of the Sheriff's confirmed plans, tension returning]**

The messenger arrived two days later — a thin, road-worn woman Ellen had apparently vouched for, carrying news with the particular grim reluctance of someone who wished, quite plainly, that she had nothing to report at all.

"The Sheriff's called a proper muster," she said, once she'd caught her breath. "Not scouts, not a search party this time. Men. A great many of them, gathering at Nottingham, with orders — my source was certain of this part — to comb Sherwood properly, top to bottom, however long it takes."

"When?" Marian asked, already doing the arithmetic she always did when arithmetic was the only useful response available.

"Within the fortnight, near as anyone can tell. He's done asking questions." The messenger's expression was grim, sympathetic, and entirely without false comfort. "He means to end this properly, this time. Whatever it costs him."

*(new text page)*

The mood that settled over the camp afterward had none of the earlier evening's ease left in it — Long John went very quiet, Will's hand found his sleeve and stayed there, and even Brother Crumb, for once, didn't reach for food as a first response to difficult news.

"Well," said Robin, eventually, into the heavy silence, with more resolve in his voice than the moment strictly should have allowed him. "We knew this was coming. We simply didn't know it was coming quite this soon."

"No," Marian agreed, and found, glancing once at Pip and then at Thistlewood — the two people in this camp who understood, more completely than anyone else present, exactly what they were actually facing — a strange, steadying comfort in not being the only one who knew the full shape of what was ahead. "But we're not facing it the way we were a month ago, either. That has to count for something."

---

**[PLATE 5 — closing: the gang and the three-way alliance settling in for what's coming, resolve and uncertainty in equal measure, story ending on tension rather than resolution]**

Nobody slept especially well that night, though nobody said as much aloud, each of them keeping their own private vigil against a threat that had finally, after months of rumor and near-miss, acquired a proper shape and a proper timeline.

Marian found Thistlewood and Pip sitting together near the treeline, close enough to speak quietly, and settled beside them without needing to ask permission.

"A fortnight," she said.

"A fortnight," Thistlewood agreed. "Which is either a great deal of time or none at all, depending entirely on how well we use it."

"Then we'd best use it well," said Pip, with a steadiness that belied how young she still, in some ways, remained.

Marian looked between the two of them — the wizard who'd spent years quietly protecting a boy who'd never know it, and the young woman who'd spent even longer protecting herself — and felt, for the first time since any of this had started, something that wasn't quite fear and wasn't quite hope, but sat somewhere honest in the space between them.

"Then let's begin," she said, and none of them moved from the fire for a long while yet, three people who knew the truth, sitting quietly together in the dark, while the rest of the camp slept on, unknowing, and the Sheriff's fortnight began, somewhere beyond the trees, to count itself down.
