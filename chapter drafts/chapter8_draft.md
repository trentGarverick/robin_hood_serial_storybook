# Story: Six Degrees of Sherwood
## Part Eight: A Council of Sorts

*(Working title — happy to change. Third and final of the closing parts — this wraps up Story 1.)*

---

**[PLATE 1 — the five of them back at Sherwood Camp, properly settled now, a real fire, real belongings, the sense of a home rather than a stopover]**

It took them the better part of a week to admit, even to themselves, that they had stopped merely surviving and started, in some unplanned and faintly alarming way, *settling*. The camp had acquired a proper fire pit. Marian's tools had found a permanent corner. Brother Crumb had, through means nobody had thought to question too closely, acquired a second cooking pot. It was, by any reasonable measure, a home — which was precisely the sort of thing none of them had quite worked up the nerve to say aloud.

It was Marian, in the end, who said it plainly, because someone generally had to.

"We ought to decide what we're actually doing," she said, "before we accidentally become a tradition instead of a plan."

---

*(new text page)*

"We're doing what we've been doing," said Robin, from his usual spot too close to the fire. "Helping where we find trouble."

"That's not a plan," said Marian. "That's a habit that occasionally hasn't gotten us killed."

"It's worked so far."

"So has falling down stairs, for some people. It's not a *strategy*." She settled onto a log with the particular posture of a woman calling a meeting to order whether anyone else had agreed to attend one. "So. What are we. Properly. Say it out loud, and let's see if it survives contact with the rest of us."

---

**[PLATE 2 — the debate beginning, each character pitching a different idea, animated disagreement**]

"Justice," said Robin, without much hesitation, "for people the law's stopped bothering to protect."

"That's a feeling," said Will, "not a plan. Feelings don't pay for anything." He examined his sleeve, found it acceptable, and continued. "I say we go where the money already is. Merchants on the Nottingham road carry more coin in an afternoon than that whole geese village sees in a year. Take from people who can afford the loss."

"That's just thievery with a nicer coat on," said Long John, uneasily.

"Most good ideas are," said Will, entirely unbothered.

---

*(new text page)*

"Feed people," said Brother Crumb, simply, not looking up from whatever he was stirring. "That's the whole of it, so far as I can tell. Everything else is just the means of getting there. Doesn't much matter whether it's coin or grain or a warped arrow sold cheap — hungry people stop being hungry, or they don't. That's the measure I'd use."

"That's a fine principle," said Marian, "and a terrible plan, because it tells us nothing about *where* or *how* or what we do the day the Sheriff's men are three fields over and closing."

"I didn't say it was the whole plan," said Crumb, mildly. "I said it was the measure. You lot can argue about the plan. I'll keep cooking."

---

**[PLATE 3 — Long John voicing caution, the group's dynamic visible — differing priorities, real affection underneath the argument]**

"Can I say the thing nobody wants said?" Long John asked, and did not wait for permission. "We nearly lost Robin. Properly lost him, not 'had a bad afternoon' lost him. Whatever we decide we're doing, I'd like it to account for the fact that some of us are considerably easier to kill than luck generally allows for."

The fire popped. Nobody argued with him, which was its own kind of answer.

"Noted," said Marian, more gently than she'd said anything yet. "Genuinely. That goes in the plan too — whatever the plan turns out to be."

---

*(new text page)*

"So we've got justice, profit, dinner, and staying alive," said Will, counting them off on his fingers with theatrical patience. "Four excellent, entirely incompatible reasons to do four completely different things tomorrow morning."

"Welcome to leadership," said Marian.

"I don't recall volunteering for leadership."

"Nobody ever does," said Marian. "It happens to you regardless, the moment you're the one keeping count."

Robin, who had been quiet for a while, turning something over that had nothing to do with the conversation, looked up. "Then maybe that's the answer. Not one reason. All of them, depending on the week — so long as somebody's keeping count of which one we're serving, and why."

There was a pause while everyone considered whether that was wisdom or simply an archer refusing to commit to a target, and settled, by unspoken agreement, on not deciding which.

---

**[PLATE 4 — a quieter beat: someone notices the still-unopened letter, Robin deflects, the thread stays open]**

It was Marian, sharp-eyed as ever, who noticed the corner of folded parchment still tucked, months later, into the lining of Robin's pack — the same seal, the same undisturbed wax, carried the entire distance from the cottage he no longer called home to a camp that increasingly was one.

"You still haven't opened that," she said. Not a question.

Robin's hand found the pack without quite meaning to. "No."

"Going to?"

"Eventually." He said it the way a man says a thing he's said to himself a great many times without believing it any more each time. "It's not — it isn't urgent. Whatever's in it will still be in it next month."

Marian looked at him for a moment longer than the answer strictly required, filed it away with everything else she was quietly keeping track of these days, and let it go without pressing further, which was, in its own way, its own small kindness.

---

*(new text page)*

"You'll open it when you open it," she said instead. "I'm not going to be the one who tells you when that is."

"That's uncharacteristically patient of you."

"Don't get used to it," said Marian, and the corner of her mouth did something that took most of the sting out of it.

---

**[PLATE 5 — a traveler or messenger passing through camp with news, mentioning the Sheriff of Nottingham has started taking notice, a forward-looking tease]**

The traveler who found their fire that evening was headed north, thin and road-worn, grateful for the offered bread and considerably more talkative than the situation strictly required once Brother Crumb had fed him.

"You'll want to be careful, out this way," he said, between mouthfuls. "There's talk in Nottingham. Sheriff's taken a personal interest — tax men losing their nerve, highwaymen routed by an old woodsman nobody can properly describe, a fletcher's arrows that never quite fly true for his soldiers. He's started asking questions. Out loud. To people who don't much like being asked."

"Is that meant to frighten us?" Will asked, with the particular bravado of a man who was, in fact, slightly frightened.

"Meant to inform you," said the traveler, "what you do with the information's your own business," and went back to the bread with the single-minded focus of a man who had said his piece and considered the matter closed.

---

Nobody said much for a while after he'd gone, each of them turning the news over in their own particular way — Long John frowning at the fire, Will checking his sleeve for reasons that had nothing to do with his sleeve, Brother Crumb, notably, not stopping his cooking for even a moment, as though the news changed nothing about what needed doing regardless of who was watching.

"Well," said Marian eventually, into the quiet. "There's your answer, in a way. Whatever we decide we're doing — we're doing it with someone paying proper attention now. That changes the arithmetic."

"Does it change what we do?" Robin asked.

"No," Marian admitted, after a moment. "Just how carefully we ought to be doing it."

---

**[PLATE 6 — closing image: the five of them around the fire, no single decision made but a clear sense of unity and purpose, warm and hopeful]**

They never did settle it, not properly — not that night, and if Robin was honest with himself, probably not for a good while yet. Justice and profit and dinner and staying alive all sat uneasily around the same fire, same as the five of them did, none of them quite willing to yield the argument and none of them, notably, willing to leave it either.

"So," said Will, eventually, into the companionable quiet that had settled over the unresolved argument. "Tomorrow, then. What are we?"

"Whatever trouble needs us to be," said Robin, "until we work out something cleverer."

"That's not a plan," said Marian, for what felt like the tenth time that evening — but she said it fondly this time, and didn't argue further, and settled back against a log that had, somewhere in the last week, become unmistakably hers.

Somewhere above them, unnoticed, a goat regarded the lot of them with placid, unreadable patience, chewing on something that had recently been someone's second-best stocking, entirely uncaught, exactly as always.
