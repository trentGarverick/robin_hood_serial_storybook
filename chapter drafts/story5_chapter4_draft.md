# Story 5: The Sheriff's Reckoning
## Part Four: The Trap Springs Shut

*(Working title — happy to change. This chapter leans more serious than most — a real setback.)*

---

**[PLATE 1 — the crew on a routine errand to Ashcombe, lighter mood, warmth before the storm]**

Ellen had sent word that the Ashcombe well needed proper repair before winter, and Robin had volunteered them for the work with the particular cheerful enthusiasm of a man glad of an excuse to do something straightforwardly useful after weeks of nothing but watching and waiting.

"It's good for morale," he said, hefting a coil of rope over one shoulder. "Doing something that isn't scouting. Something that just... helps."

"It's good for morale," Marian agreed, "and it's also the first time in a fortnight any of us have left camp without checking the treeline three times first. Try not to let the one make you careless about the other."

"I'm never careless," Robin said, with the wounded dignity of a man who was, on balance, frequently careless, and knew it.

The work went well, in the end — Long John's strength doing most of the heavy lifting, Will charming half of Ashcombe into helping with the lighter tasks, Brother Crumb feeding everyone involved with his usual generous excess. Hob and Wat trailed after Robin the entire afternoon with the particular devoted uselessness of boys who wanted very much to help and mostly succeeded in handing him the wrong tool at the wrong moment.

"You're good at this," Hob said, watching Robin work a stubborn stone loose from the well's collapsed rim.

"I'm good at very little," Robin said, cheerfully, "but I'll take this as a fair exception."

It was, everyone would later agree, a genuinely pleasant afternoon, right up until it wasn't.

---

**[PLATE 2 — the ambush beginning: Blackwood's disciplined soldiers appearing from multiple directions, the trap clearly planned]**

The soldiers didn't announce themselves with the usual crash of undergrowth that had, by now, become a reasonably reliable warning. They simply were there, all at once, emerging from three separate directions with the coordinated precision of men who had been given a map rather than a guess — cutting off the road, the treeline, and the well itself in the same efficient motion.

"That's not luck," Marian said, already moving, already understanding with cold clarity exactly what she was looking at. "That's a plan. Someone told them precisely where to be."

"Vane," Robin said, grim, spotting the familiar overdressed figure hanging back at the formation's edge, close enough to point and identify, careful enough to stay well clear of the actual fighting.

---

*(new text page)*

"Marian Fletcher," Vane called out, with real relish, pointing directly at her across the chaos already breaking out around the well. "The archer's beside her — that's Loxby. The giant's the blacksmith. Take them alive, Captain, the Sheriff will want—"

Whatever the Sheriff would want was lost in the noise of actual fighting, Blackwood's soldiers advancing with the same disciplined efficiency the crew had watched from a distant ridge and found considerably more alarming up close, where distance no longer softened any of it.

"Get the boys clear!" Marian shouted, already fighting, already doing the desperate arithmetic of a losing position. "Hob! Wat! Now!"

---

**[PLATE 3 — the fight turning desperate: the crew overwhelmed by superior numbers and coordination, real danger]**

What followed bore no resemblance to any fight they'd survived so far — no lucky misdirection, no fortunate accident arriving at precisely the needed moment, simply five people and considerably more than five trained soldiers, and the grim mathematics of that imbalance asserting itself with brutal, uncomplicated clarity.

Long John went down first, not from any single blow but from sheer accumulated weight, three men bearing him to the ground through effort rather than cleverness. Will fought with real desperation beside him, sleeve forgotten entirely, landing blows with none of his usual careless grace and considerably more raw necessity.

"Robin!" Marian's voice, somewhere behind him, cracking with an urgency he'd never heard from her before. "Robin, behind you—"

---

*(new text page)*

He turned too slowly. The blow that took him down wasn't meant to kill — Vane's instructions had been explicit on that point, and Blackwood's men followed orders with the same disciplined precision they did everything else — but it was more than sufficient to put him on the ground, dazed, weaponless, and entirely unable to prevent the hands that closed around his arms a moment later.

"No—" Marian was fighting toward him now, desperate, careless of her own safety in a way she never allowed herself to be, but the gap between them had become, in the space of a few brutal seconds, considerably wider than she could close in time.

"Marian, go!" Robin shouted, the words costing him what little breath he had left. "Get the others out! Go!"

---

**[PLATE 4 — Robin's capture: the moment itself, stark and serious, the others forced to retreat]**

They took him at a run, Blackwood's soldiers falling back into disciplined formation around their prize with the efficient speed of men who had trained for exactly this outcome, and there was nothing — not luck, not improvisation, not five months of accumulated small miracles — that any of them could do to stop it from happening.

"Fall back!" Marian's voice again, ragged now, the command costing her more than any tactical decision she'd ever had to make. "Long John — Will — fall back, now, there's nothing left to save here by staying—"

They fell back. It was, by any honest measure, the only choice available to them, and it felt, to every single person making it, like the worst decision any of them had made since this had all started.

---

*(new text page)*

Vane watched them go with an expression of pure, uncomplicated satisfaction, already turning to follow Blackwood's retreating column, already savoring — visibly, unbearably — a victory that had cost him nothing and given him everything he'd wanted since a strongbox had first gone missing from his manor.

"One down," he called after the fleeing crew, cheerful and cruel in equal measure. "Only four left to find, and I've a very good memory for faces."

Nobody answered him. Nobody had breath left to spare for it.

---

**[PLATE 5 — aftermath: the devastated crew regrouping, grief and resolve, Thistlewood's reaction]**

They reached the treeline in ragged, breathless disarray, and it was there, finally, that the full weight of what had actually happened caught up with them all at once — Long John sitting heavily against a tree, favoring a shoulder that had taken the worst of the soldiers' weight; Will staring at his ruined sleeve without seeing it; Marian standing rigid and silent, the particular silence of a woman refusing, by sheer force of will, to let herself fall apart until there was time to properly afford it.

Thistlewood found them within the hour, Pip close behind him, and the expression on his face when Marian told him — flat, controlled, sparing nothing — was unlike anything any of them had seen from him before.

---

*(new text page)*

"They have Robin," Thistlewood repeated, quietly, as though saying it aloud might make it settle into something he could actually work with rather than simply absorb.

"Yes," Marian said.

He was silent for a long moment, and something moved behind his eyes that none of them — save perhaps Marian, who understood exactly what weight that silence actually carried — could have properly named.

"Then we're going to need considerably more than luck to get him back," he said, finally, and there was, underneath the words, something that had stopped sounding entirely like an old man's careful understatement. "I made a promise a long time ago that I intend to keep. Whatever it costs to keep it."

He looked at each of them in turn — Marian, Will, Long John, Brother Crumb, Pip beside him, quiet and steady — and something settled in his expression that looked considerably less like caution than it had an hour before.

"We begin now," he said. "All of it. However much of myself that requires."
