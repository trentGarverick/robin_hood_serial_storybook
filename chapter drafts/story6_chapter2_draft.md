# Story 6: A Goat of Some Distinction
## Part Two: Best in Show

*(Working title — happy to change.)*

---

**[PLATE 1 — arriving at the festival grounds to find Gideon on display, clearly enjoying the attention]**

They found him at the center of a small, admiring crowd, ribbon pinned with real ceremony to a length of rope around his neck, accepting the attention of a dozen strangers with the serene, unhurried dignity of a creature who had always privately suspected this was roughly the treatment he deserved.

*"He's insufferable,"* Grimalkin observed, arriving at Marian's shoulder in time to witness the full scope of it. *"Look at him. He's practically posing."*

*"He's happy,"* Barley said, delighted on Gideon's behalf in a way that suggested he'd forgotten entirely about the actual crisis at hand. *"Look how happy he is! Can we get ribbons? I want to know what a ribbon feels like."*

"Gideon," Marian called, cutting through the crowd. "We're here. You can stop performing now."

Gideon, for his part, did not stop performing.

*"This is the finest afternoon of my life,"* Gideon informed the other two, with the particular satisfaction of an animal who had, after years of quiet competence, finally received his due. *"Do you understand what these people are saying about me? A judge actually touched my flank and made a noise like he'd found something remarkable. I intend to remember this."*

*"You're going to be unbearable about it for months,"* Grimalkin said.

*"Years, probably,"* Gideon agreed, entirely without shame. *"I've earned it."*

Robin, watching this exchange with no idea it was happening, simply saw a goat looking uncommonly pleased with himself, and found that somehow the least surprising part of the entire afternoon.

---

**[PLATE 2 — meeting Reeve Cobb, his genuine pride and procedural resistance]**

Reeve Aldous Cobb turned out to be a round, cheerful man whose enthusiasm for his festival occupied roughly the same volume and space as his actual body, and whose face, upon learning that his prize entry had an owner after all, went through several rapid stages of disappointment before settling, gamely, on something resembling professional courtesy.

"You understand my position," he said, wringing his hands with real distress. "He's the finest entry we've had in a decade. Properly registered, properly judged — there's a *process*, you understand, and simply removing him mid-judging would set an extremely awkward precedent for next year's festival."

"He's our goat, Reeve Cobb," Marian said, with the particular patience of a woman who had negotiated with considerably more difficult men than this. "There's no precedent to set. He wandered off. We'd like him back."

"Of course, of course," Cobb said, not moving to release anything at all. "Simply — let me consult the ledger. There are *forms*."

It was at precisely this unfortunate moment that Widow Tillet arrived, a formidable woman of indeterminate but considerable age, leading a goat of her own — a handsome, clearly pampered animal named Bartholomew, whom she introduced with the same pride most people reserved for grandchildren.

"That's the newcomer, is it," she said, looking Gideon over with the flat, assessing hostility of a woman who had won this category for eleven consecutive years and had no intention of losing it to a stranger in year twelve. "Rather convenient, an owner turning up right when the judging's going against Bartholomew."

"He's not *against* anyone," Robin said, reasonably. "We simply lost track of him this morning."

"So you say," said Widow Tillet, in the tone of a woman who did not, in fact, say.

---

**[PLATE 3 — the nonsense cant escalating, invented terms deployed, extra comedy from Cobb and Tillet's confusion]**

"We really must insist," Marian said, growing visibly, carefully impatient with the delay, "before this becomes a proper bishop's-bonnet of an afternoon."

Robin frowned. "A bishop's what?"

"*Elephant*, obviously," Will said, entirely straight-faced. "Everyone knows that one."

"That doesn't even rhyme."

"Cant doesn't have to rhyme *every* time, Robin," Marian said, with the serene confidence of a woman inventing rules faster than she could be caught inventing them. "Some of the older ones are simply traditional."

Reeve Cobb, listening to this exchange with the polite, faintly lost expression of a man determined not to reveal his own ignorance, nodded along as though he too understood perfectly.

"Turnip's cousin," Brother Crumb added, warming to the game with visible relish, "before Cobb here finds his forms."

"His *what*?" Robin asked, though the exhaustion in his voice suggested he'd mostly stopped expecting a real answer.

"*Tomorrow*," said Long John, who had, by this point, simply committed fully to whatever the group decided reality was going to be for the afternoon.

Widow Tillet, catching the rhythm of it and apparently deciding she rather liked the sound of belonging to a joke she didn't understand, added, with real conviction: "Well, I never wanted to be a proper kettle's-spout about the whole business myself."

"*Drought*," Marian translated automatically, then paused, genuinely startled, because that one had, entirely by accident, actually worked.

---

**[PLATE 4 — Gideon's continued enjoyment, a complication with the judging schedule, tension building toward Part Three]**

*"This is extraordinary,"* Grimalkin said, watching the humans construct an entire fictional dialect in real time purely to torment one man. *"They've been doing this for an hour. I don't think even they remember which parts are real anymore."*

*"I believe the elephant one,"* Barley said, earnestly. *"It sounds like something that should be true."*

*"It isn't.*"

*"I know. I still believe it."*

Reeve Cobb, meanwhile, had located his forms, examined them with real concern, and arrived, with evident reluctance, at a piece of news that settled the whole argument rather more firmly than any amount of invented slang had managed.

"The judging concludes tomorrow morning," he said. "Best in Show is decided then. I'm afraid the entries can't be withdrawn before the category's properly finished — not without forfeiting the whole competition's results, which the committee would take rather badly."

"Tomorrow morning," Marian repeated, flat.

"Tomorrow morning," Cobb confirmed, apologetic but immovable. "I truly am sorry for the inconvenience. He really is a magnificent animal."

Widow Tillet, hearing this reprieve, allowed herself something close to a smile for the first time since her arrival. "Well," she said, "I suppose we'll simply have to see how the judging actually goes, won't we."

Marian looked at Gideon — currently accepting a fresh round of admiring pats with the serene confidence of a creature who had, by every available account, no intention whatsoever of losing tomorrow's judging even if he'd wanted to — and felt the particular sinking certainty of a woman who understood, completely, that this was no longer a simple matter of walking home.

"Tomorrow morning," she said again, mostly to herself, and went to go find somewhere for all of them to sleep.
